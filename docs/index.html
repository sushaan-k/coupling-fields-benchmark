Abstract

Background. Linked single-cell assays resolve cross-modal dependence because they measure molecular states in the same cell. A recipient cohort may retain the marginal state counts of both assays without preserving cell-level pairing. Its joint table is then unobserved and cannot be recovered from the margins alone.

Results. We formulate prediction from paired source donors and unpaired recipient margins. Penalized exact conditional likelihood estimates donor-specific log odds and a shared population interaction; the predicted table is the conditional mean at the recipient margins. This table uniquely minimizes expected multinomial deviance under the conditional model, and a finite-sample bound separates source estimation error from recipient association drift. Using RNA detection and within-donor median-ranked surface-protein states in 512 cells per donor, a model fitted on 36 Cambridge donors achieved mean deviance per cell 0.01220 in 56 Newcastle donors. Paule-Mandel random-effects transfer of the signed-root deviance statistic reached 0.01478, a 17.46% reduction for the conditional estimator (paired-donor 95% confidence interval for the loss difference, -0.00413 to -0.00080; 50/56 donors favorable). Mean deviance after source linkage was destroyed was 0.02274. In post hoc fitted-baseline analyses, the estimator reduced deviance by 6.18% relative to the common-effect conditional estimate (95% confidence interval, 3.84% to 8.47%). Across 128 ground-truth simulations, conditioning absorbed recipient margin shift, and donor shrinkage improved prediction when source interactions were heterogeneous.

Conclusions. Fixed-margin prediction reconstructs an unobserved cross-modal joint table from linked source donors and recipient aggregate counts. The held-site result supports transfer of RNA-surface-protein dependence for the studied marker panel, and the simulations identify when conditioning and donor shrinkage improve prediction.

Background

Linked single-cell assays measure RNA, chromatin, proteins, molecular age, and lineage in the same cell or experimentally related unit. Joint latent-variable models integrate paired measurements or predict a missing modality. We consider a different information boundary: source donors have paired measurements, whereas a recipient provides only the aggregate state counts for each assay. The target is the recipient joint table that cell-level pairing would have revealed.

Marginal frequencies change with cell composition, activation, sequencing depth, and assay scale. Copying a source table therefore transfers abundance together with dependence. Signed Pearson and likelihood-ratio statistics summarize departure from independence, but their scale and attainable range depend on the margins. A statistic pooled across donors can consequently change meaning at recipient margins.

Log-linear interactions, conditional odds-ratio estimation, and random-effects models are established tools for observed contingency tables. We join them in a predictive design for source-paired, recipient-unpaired studies. The method learns dependence from stratified source tables, combines it with each recipient's margins, and returns the conditional mean joint table.

Association coordinates

For ordered marker pair e, let Pe be a positive joint-probability table for linked states U and V. Orthonormal contrast matrices remove the row and column components of its log table. We call the resulting centered log-linear interaction the coupling field.

For a binary table, the coupling field is one-half of the log odds ratio. This field contains all log-linear interaction degrees of freedom, is invariant to positive row or column tilts, and vanishes exactly at independence.

Classical comparisons and controls

The matched statistic comparator transfers either the signed root Pearson statistic or the signed root likelihood-ratio deviance from the row-plus-column independence model. We normalized statistics by table size, pooled them across source donors, rescaled them for each recipient, and inverted them at its margins. The Stephenson protocol used Paule-Mandel random-effects pooling. Both methods received the same recipient margins without recipient pairings.

The destroyed-link control permuted complete protein profiles within each donor before joint tables were formed. This preserved RNA and protein margins and dependence within the protein panel but removed cell-level RNA-protein linkage. We refitted the selected conditional estimator to the permuted tables. Fixed-margin independence provided a further control.

Prespecified held evaluation

The Stephenson analysis plan fixed eligibility, donor allocation, cell selection, state definitions, marker panels, tuning grids, comparators, loss, uncertainty, and success criteria before the Newcastle pairing outcomes were observed. The Cambridge pilot selected the final configuration from these grids, after which all Cambridge development donors supplied the final fit. Predictions used Newcastle margins alone and were recorded before RNA-protein pairing was restored for evaluation.

Association transferred across recruitment sites

Across 56 Newcastle donors, the hierarchical conditional estimator reached mean deviance 0.01220, compared with 0.01478 for signed-root deviance-statistic transfer. The corresponding 17.46% reduction had a paired-donor loss-difference interval of -0.00413 to -0.00080. The conditional estimate was better in 50/56 donors. All 36 Cambridge development donors supplied the final source fit after pilot selection.

The estimator also reduced deviance by 46.36% relative to the destroyed-link fit of 0.02274 (paired-donor interval -0.01323 to -0.00749; 51/56 donors favorable). In post hoc fitted-baseline analyses, it improved on the common-effect conditional estimate by 6.18% and on pooled-table log odds with conditional reconstruction by 3.39%.

Conditioning and donor shrinkage separated distinct errors

The factorial simulation separated donor heterogeneity from margin shift. With heterogeneous donor effects and source-like margins, the hierarchical estimator reached deviance 0.01493, compared with 0.01521 for the common-effect conditional estimate. The reduction was 1.85% (95% interval 1.52% to 2.18%). After the recipient margins shifted, conditional transport reduced deviance by 65.49% (63.02% to 67.72%) relative to raw signed-root deviance-statistic transfer.

Discussion

Recipient margins and source heterogeneity govern different parts of the prediction. Fixed-margin reconstruction absorbed abundance shifts in simulation and transferred RNA-surface-protein dependence from Cambridge to Newcastle without copying source composition. Donor shrinkage improved on common-effect conditional estimation at the held site and under simulated heterogeneity. Its advantage disappeared when simulated source effects were homogeneous, locating the gain in donor pooling rather than in the interaction coordinate alone.

Signed-statistic transfer was the prespecified comparator because it uses exactly the available information: linked source tables and unpaired recipient margins. Models such as totalVI and MultiVI solve missing-modality problems from paired recipient observations or cell-level covariates and cannot be fitted under this recipient-unpaired protocol without changing the task. The common-effect conditional estimate provides the closest fitted classical comparison under the same information boundary; hierarchical pooling reduced its held-site deviance by 6.18%.

Limitations

The held confirmation is an observational RNA-surface-protein study and does not establish perturbation causality, molecular binding, or a regulatory mechanism. It uses binary states, 512 cells per donor, and nine RNA and nine protein markers. Within-donor median ranks fix each recipient protein margin at 256/256. The held experiment therefore evaluates varying RNA prevalence and site-dependent association at one protein discretization; it does not test shifts in protein prevalence, natural protein thresholds, other quantiles, or other cell budgets. The 81 pairwise tables within a donor reuse cells and marker vectors. Broader panels, technologies, tissues, state resolutions, and marker sets require independent replication.

The held-site selection assigned no weight to graph regularization, so the empirical claim concerns hierarchical conditional transfer without graph or hypergraph smoothing. The prespecified Paule-Mandel comparator pools signed statistics rather than log odds; a separate random-effects log-linear comparator was not fitted for Stephenson. The fitted-interaction comparisons were specified after the held result and are descriptive. Additional file 1 reports the complete study sequence, including unsuccessful transfers and a cohort analysis corrected after outcome access.

Conclusions

Fixed-margin prediction reconstructs cell-level cross-modal dependence from linked source donors and recipient aggregate counts. In the prespecified held-site experiment, hierarchical conditional estimation improved on matched random-effects statistic transfer and destroyed source linkage. Recipient margins address abundance shift, and source heterogeneity determines the value of donor shrinkage.
