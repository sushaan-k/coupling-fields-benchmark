\section{Coupling coordinates}

\subsection{Definition}

Let $P\in(0,1)^{r\times s}$ be a joint probability table, and let $B_r\in\R^{r\times(r-1)}$ and $B_s\in\R^{s\times(s-1)}$ have orthonormal columns spanning the complements of the all-ones vectors. The population coupling field is
\begin{equation}
  \field(P)=B_r^{\mathsf T}\log(P)B_s.
  \label{eq:s-field}
\end{equation}
We use Helmert bases. Other orthonormal contrast bases rotate the $(r-1)(s-1)$ coordinates without changing Frobenius norms or pairwise Frobenius distances.

\begin{proposition}[Marginal invariance and independence]
For positive vectors $a\in\R^r$ and $b\in\R^s$, define
\[
P'=\frac{\operatorname{diag}(a)P\operatorname{diag}(b)}
{\sum_{u,v}a_uP_{uv}b_v}.
\]
Then $\field(P')=\field(P)$. Moreover, $\field(P)=0$ if and only if $P$ is an independent joint law.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing $c$ for the normalizing constant gives
\[
\log P'=\log P+(\log a)\mathbf 1_s^{\mathsf T}
+\mathbf 1_r(\log b)^{\mathsf T}-\log(c)\mathbf 1_r\mathbf 1_s^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The added terms are annihilated on the left by $B_r^{\mathsf T}$ or on the right by $B_s$, proving invariance. Factorization $P_{uv}=p_uq_v$ makes $\log P$ a sum of row and column terms, so $\field(P)=0$. Conversely, $\field(P)=0$ removes the interaction component in the orthogonal decomposition into row and column terms. Hence $\log P_{uv}=\alpha_u+\beta_v$, and $P$ factorizes after normalization.
\end{proof}

\section{Binary fixed-margin estimator}

The primary estimator conditions binary RNA-protein tables on their observed margins, which eliminates nuisance marginal parameters. Predictions use the recipient margins without a pseudocount.

\subsection{Binary scaling identity}

For
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}p_{00}&p_{01}\\p_{10}&p_{11}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B_2=2^{-1/2}(1,-1)^{\mathsf T},
\]
Eq.~\eqref{eq:s-field} is scalar and satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \field(P)=\frac12\log\frac{p_{00}p_{11}}{p_{01}p_{10}}.
  \label{eq:binary-helmert}
\end{equation}
The conventional log odds ratio is therefore $\theta=2\field(P)$. Estimation uses $\theta$; reported field coordinates use the Helmert scale in Eq.~\eqref{eq:binary-helmert}.

\subsection{Fixed-margin likelihood}

For donor $d$ and ordered RNA-protein pair $e$, write the $2\times2$ table as
\[
N_{de}=\begin{pmatrix}
A_{de}&r_{0,de}-A_{de}\\
c_{0,de}-A_{de}&n_{de}-r_{0,de}-c_{0,de}+A_{de}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $r_{0,de}$ and $c_{0,de}$ are the first row and column totals and $c_{1,de}=n_{de}-c_{0,de}$. Conditional on these margins,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr_{\theta_{de}}(A=a\mid r_0,c_0,n)
 =\frac{\binom{c_0}{a}\binom{c_1}{r_0-a}\exp(\theta_{de}a)}
 {Z_{de}(\theta_{de})},
 \quad
 Z_{de}(\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_{de}}
 \binom{c_0}{a}\binom{c_1}{r_0-a}\exp(\theta a),
 \label{eq:conditional-nchg}
\end{equation}
on the feasible integer support $\mathcal A_{de}$. Omitting a constant independent of $\theta$, the negative log likelihood is
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{de}(\theta)=\log Z_{de}(\theta)-A_{de}\theta,
 \qquad
 \ell'_{de}(\theta)=\E_\theta[A]-A_{de},
 \qquad
 \ell''_{de}(\theta)=\operatorname{Var}_\theta(A).
 \label{eq:conditional-derivatives}
\end{equation}
An informative table has at least two values in $\mathcal A_{de}$, making $\ell''_{de}>0$ and the conditional odds ratio identifiable.

Hierarchical conditional and random-effects log-linear models share odds-ratio information across $2\times2$ strata~\cite{liao1999,coullagresti2003,stijnen2010}. We estimate donor-specific and population log odds jointly across the entity panel, then predict recipient tables at their observed margins.

\subsection{Hierarchical transfer objective}

Let $D$ denote source donors, $E$ the ordered marker pairs, $\theta_{de}$ a donor-specific log odds ratio, and $\mu_e$ the transferable population log odds. Two marker hypergraphs induce normalized Laplacians $L_R$ and $L_A$ for the RNA and protein axes. Their product-space penalty is
\begin{equation}
 L=L_R\otimes I+I\otimes L_A.
 \label{eq:product-laplacian}
\end{equation}
The estimator minimizes the penalized composite conditional objective
\begin{equation}
 Q(\theta,\mu)=\frac1D\sum_{d=1}^{D}\sum_{e=1}^{E}\ell_{de}(\theta_{de})
 +\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{d,e}(\theta_{de}-\mu_e)^2
 +\frac{\rho}{2}\norm{\mu}_2^2
 +\frac{\tau}{2}\mu^{\mathsf T}L\mu.
 \label{eq:exact-objective}
\end{equation}
The likelihood sum is composite because entity pairs within a donor share cells and markers. We express the penalty grid through dimensionless inputs
\[
 \gamma=\eta s_\eta/D,
 \qquad \rho=\lambda_0s_\mu,
 \qquad \tau=\lambda_Gs_\mu,
\]
where $s_\eta$ is the median null Fisher information across informative donor-entity tables and $s_\mu$ is the median information after donor averaging. This scaling makes the grid invariant to duplication of the complete donor panel.

Its gradients are
\begin{align}
 \frac{\partial Q}{\partial\theta_{de}}
 &=\frac{\E_{\theta_{de}}[A]-A_{de}}{D}
   +\gamma(\theta_{de}-\mu_e),\\
 \nabla_\mu Q
 &=-\gamma\sum_d(\theta_d-\mu)+\rho\mu+\tau L\mu.
 \label{eq:exact-gradients}
\end{align}
The Hessian has diagonal $\theta$ block
$\operatorname{diag}\{\operatorname{Var}_{\theta_{de}}(A)/D+\gamma\}$,
$\theta$-$\mu$ entries $-\gamma$, and $\mu$ block
$D\gamma I+\rho I+\tau L$. Its quadratic form is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{d,e}\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{\theta_{de}}(A)}{D}u_{de}^2
 +\gamma\sum_{d,e}(u_{de}-v_e)^2
 +\rho\norm{v}_2^2+\tau v^{\mathsf T}Lv.
 \label{eq:exact-hessian-form}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite identifiable fit]
If $\gamma>0$, $\rho>0$, and $\tau\ge0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-objective} has a unique finite minimizer. For every informative donor-entity table, its unpenalized conditional parameter is identifiable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each likelihood term is convex by Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-derivatives}, and the product Laplacian is positive semidefinite. Equality to zero in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-hessian-form} requires $u_{de}=v_e$ through the $\gamma$ term and $v=0$ through the $\rho$ term, hence $u=0$. The objective is strictly convex and coercive. The final statement follows from $\ell''_{de}>0$ on every informative support.
\end{proof}

Newton updates eliminate donor-specific parameters by the Schur complement. The population diagonal then receives
\[
 \sum_d\frac{\gamma\{\operatorname{Var}_{\theta_{de}}(A)/D\}}
 {\gamma+\operatorname{Var}_{\theta_{de}}(A)/D}
\]
in addition to $\rho I+\tau L$. Backtracking enforces descent, and fits that exceed prespecified gradient-norm or condition-number thresholds are rejected.

For a recipient donor, Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-nchg} with log odds $\alpha\widehat\mu_e$ and the recipient margins yields a conditional-expectation prediction that preserves both margins. Development donors determine $\alpha$ and all penalties.

\subsection{Transfer risk and recipient drift}

To separate source estimation error from recipient variation, fix informative margins $M=(r_0,r_1,c_0,c_1)$ with total $N$ and endpoints
\[
  L_M=\max(0,r_0+c_0-N),
  \qquad
  U_M=\min(r_0,c_0)
\]
for the feasible upper-left count. Write Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-nchg} as
\[
  p_\theta(a\mid M)=h_M(a)\exp\{\theta a-\psi_M(\theta)\},
  \qquad
  I_M(\theta)=\psi_M''(\theta)=\operatorname{Var}_\theta(A\mid M).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-margin transfer risk]
Let $\mathcal S$ contain all source information, and suppose a recipient log odds satisfies
\[
  \theta_q=\mu+\xi_q,
  \qquad
  \E(\xi_q\mid\mathcal S,M)=0,
  \qquad
  \E(\xi_q^2\mid\mathcal S,M)=\sigma_M^2.
\]
The source estimate $\widehat\mu$ and multiplier $\alpha$ are $\mathcal S$-measurable, and the recipient prediction uses $\widehat\theta=\alpha\widehat\mu$. Assume that $\theta_q$ and $\widehat\theta$ lie almost surely in a compact interval $\mathcal T$. Define
\[
  \underline I_M=\inf_{\theta\in\mathcal T}I_M(\theta),
  \qquad
  \overline I_M=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal T}I_M(\theta).
\]
Then the conditional excess log loss
\[
  \mathcal R_M=
  \E\!\left[
    \operatorname{KL}\{p_{\theta_q}(\cdot\mid M)\,\|\,
    p_{\widehat\theta}(\cdot\mid M)\}
    \mid\mathcal S,M
  \right]
\]
satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\underline I_M}{2}
  \left\{(\alpha\widehat\mu-\mu)^2+\sigma_M^2\right\}
  \leq \mathcal R_M \leq
  \frac{\overline I_M}{2}
  \left\{(\alpha\widehat\mu-\mu)^2+\sigma_M^2\right\}.
  \label{eq:transfer-risk-bound}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $I_M(\theta)\leq(U_M-L_M)^2/4$ for every finite $\theta$, so
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal R_M\leq
  \frac{(U_M-L_M)^2}{8}
  \left\{(\alpha\widehat\mu-\mu)^2+\sigma_M^2\right\}.
  \label{eq:finite-margin-risk-bound}
\end{equation}
Divide these bounds by $N$ for excess log loss per cell.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For canonical parameters $\theta$ and $\eta$,
\[
  \operatorname{KL}(p_\theta\|p_\eta)
  =\psi_M(\eta)-\psi_M(\theta)-\psi_M'(\theta)(\eta-\theta).
\]
Taylor's theorem with integral remainder gives
\[
  \operatorname{KL}(p_\theta\|p_\eta)
  =(\eta-\theta)^2\int_0^1(1-t)
  I_M\{\theta+t(\eta-\theta)\}\,dt.
\]
Bounding the information along the segment from $\theta$ to $\eta$, substituting $\theta=\mu+\xi_q$ and $\eta=\alpha\widehat\mu$, and taking the conditional expectation prove Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer-risk-bound}. Popoviciu's inequality gives $I_M(\theta)=\operatorname{Var}_\theta(A\mid M)\leq(U_M-L_M)^2/4$, which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-margin-risk-bound}. For informative margins, $I_M$ is continuous and positive on the compact interval $\mathcal T$, hence $\underline I_M>0$.
\end{proof}

If $\alpha$ is fixed across source repetitions and $b=\E(\widehat\mu)-\mu$, the reducible term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer-risk-bound} decomposes as
\begin{equation}
  \E(\alpha\widehat\mu-\mu)^2
  =\alpha^2\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\mu)
  +\{\alpha b+(\alpha-1)\mu\}^2.
  \label{eq:source-risk-decomposition}
\end{equation}
As source estimation becomes consistent and $\alpha$ approaches one, $\sigma_M^2$ remains in the conditional log loss of a single transported parameter relative to the donor-specific conditional law. This comparison does not imply a positive lower bound on mean-table prediction regret.

\subsection{Decision rule for table prediction}

The conditional model defines a distribution over integer tables with the recipient margins. The prediction used in the paper is its mean table.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional mean minimizes expected deviance]
Let $N$ be a random contingency table with fixed margins $M$ and total $n$, and let $\overline N=\E(N\mid M)$. For any nonnegative table $Q$ with margins $M$ and $Q_{ij}>0$ whenever $\overline N_{ij}>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \E\{D(N,Q)\mid M\}-\E\{D(N,\overline N)\mid M\}
  =\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i,j:\overline N_{ij}>0}
  \overline N_{ij}\log\frac{\overline N_{ij}}{Q_{ij}}\geq0.
  \label{eq:mean-deviance-decision}
\end{equation}
The conditional mean is the unique minimizer on cells with positive conditional mean.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any admissible $Q$,
\[
  \E\{D(N,Q)\mid M\}
  =\frac{2}{n}\E\!\left(\sum_{i,j:N_{ij}>0}N_{ij}\log N_{ij}\,\middle|\,M\right)
  -\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i,j}\overline N_{ij}\log Q_{ij}.
\]
The first term is independent of $Q$. Subtracting the same expression evaluated at $\overline N$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:mean-deviance-decision}. Because $Q/n$ and $\overline N/n$ are probability tables, the right side is twice their Kullback--Leibler divergence. It is nonnegative and vanishes only when $Q=\overline N$ on the support of $\overline N$. Every table in the conditional support has margins $M$, so linearity of expectation gives the same margins to $\overline N$.
\end{proof}

For a predictive interaction law $G$, linearity gives the mean table
$Q_G=\int\E_\theta(N\mid M)\,dG(\theta)$. Proposition S4 applies to this
mixture as well as to a single interaction. The following consequence connects
error in the interaction distribution to the table loss.

\begin{corollary}[Distribution error and table prediction]
Fix binary margins $M$ with total $n>0$ and feasible upper-left counts
$\{L,\ldots,U\}$; write $w=U-L$. For probability laws $G,H$ on real log
odds with finite second moments, define
\[
 P_G(a)=\int p_\theta(a\mid M)\,dG(\theta),\qquad Q_G=\E_{P_G}(N),
\]
and define $P_H,Q_H$ analogously. Then
\begin{align*}
 \E_{P_G}\{D(N,Q_H)-D(N,Q_G)\}
 &=2\operatorname{KL}(Q_G/n\,\|\,Q_H/n)\\
 &\leq2\operatorname{KL}(P_G\,\|\,P_H)\\
 &\leq\frac{w^2}{4}W_2^2(G,H),
\end{align*}
where $W_2$ is the quadratic Wasserstein distance. Structural zero cells
contribute zero. If $w=0$, all quantities vanish. For normal laws, including
zero variances, $W_2^2(G,H)=(\mu_G-\mu_H)^2+(\sigma_G-\sigma_H)^2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Proposition S4 gives the equality. Selecting a cell category with probability
$N_{ij}(a)/n$ maps $P_G,P_H$ to $Q_G/n,Q_H/n$, so data processing gives the
first inequality. The fixed-margin log-partition function satisfies
$\psi''(\theta)\leq w^2/4$; its Taylor remainder gives
$\operatorname{KL}(p_\theta\|p_\eta)\leq w^2(\theta-\eta)^2/8$.
For any coupling $\pi$ of $G,H$, joint convexity therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_G\|P_H)
 \leq\int\operatorname{KL}(p_\theta\|p_\eta)\,d\pi
 \leq\frac{w^2}{8}\int(\theta-\eta)^2\,d\pi.
\]
Taking the infimum over couplings proves the bound. The normal-law expression
follows from their monotone quantile coupling.
\end{proof}

Different interaction laws can yield the same mean table, so positive
heterogeneity need not produce positive table regret.

\subsection{Matched signed-statistic transfer}

The matched statistic-transfer comparator uses the same binary tables, donors, splits, recipient margins, selection stage, and deviance-per-cell outcome. Stephenson pools donor statistics with the Paule--Mandel random-effects estimator~\cite{paule1982}. For determinant $D_N=N_{00}N_{11}-N_{01}N_{10}$, the signed root Pearson statistic is
\begin{equation}
 R_P=\operatorname{sign}(D_N)\sqrt{X^2}
 =D_N\sqrt{\frac{n}{r_0r_1c_0c_1}}.
\end{equation}
The signed root likelihood-ratio deviance statistic is
\begin{equation}
 R_G=\operatorname{sign}(D_N)
 \left\{2\sum_{i,j:N_{ij}>0}N_{ij}\log(N_{ij}/E_{ij})\right\}^{1/2},
 \qquad E_{ij}=r_ic_j/n.
\end{equation}
Statistics are divided by $\sqrt n$ before donor pooling, multiplied by $\alpha$, and restored to the recipient $\sqrt n$ scale before fixed-margin reconstruction. Stephenson used Paule--Mandel pooling with inverse-variance-plus-heterogeneity weights and selected the statistic, its raw or conditional-null-centered form, and $\alpha$ on the pilot. GSE239452 used equal donor means and selected a raw statistic and $\alpha$ by calibration cross-validation. COMBAT and BMMC used equal donor means and evaluated both centering rules. The hierarchical method transported conditional log odds and reconstructed the noncentral-hypergeometric expectation.

\subsection{Fitted interaction baselines}

For each entity $e$, the common-effect conditional maximum-likelihood estimate solves
\[
  \sum_d \mathbb E_{\theta_e}[A_{de}\mid m_{de}]
  =\sum_d A_{de}
\]
The implementation returns the corresponding extended estimate at a support boundary and otherwise requires an absolute score below $10^{-8}$. This estimator has no donor deviations, ridge penalty, or graph penalty.

The pooled-table baseline first sums the source-donor tables for each entity and computes the interaction coefficient from the saturated model
\[
  \log \mathbb E[N_{ij}]=\beta_0+\beta_i^{R}+\beta_j^{C}
  +\beta^{I}\mathbb 1\{i=1,j=1\}.
\]
For a $2\times2$ table, $\beta^{I}$ is the pooled sample log odds ratio, which differs from the stratified conditional estimate when donor margins vary. In the original post hoc comparison, both fitted coefficients select $\alpha$ on development donors and reconstruct held tables with Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-nchg}. The pooled method is therefore pooled-table log odds with conditional reconstruction, not a Poisson plug-in prediction.

A separate post hoc comparator fixes the same pooled saturated-model interaction and profiles recipient row and column parameters. This gives the unique positive continuous table with the recipient margins and transported odds ratio, without Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-nchg}. The original calibration and pilot donors select $\alpha$ before the interaction is refitted on all development donors.

\subsection{Conditional random-effects prediction}

For each marker pair, the classical random-effects model assumes
$\theta_{de}\sim\mathcal N(\mu_e,\tau_e^2)$ and estimates its parameters
by conditional marginal likelihood \cite{jackson2018}:
\[
 (\widehat\mu_e,\widehat\tau_e^2)
 =\arg\max_{\mu,\tau^2\geq0}
 \sum_d\log\int p(A_{de}\mid m_{de},\theta)
 \,d\mathcal N(\mu,\tau^2)(\theta).
\]
The common-effect model is the boundary $\tau_e^2=0$. Two predictions from
the fitted random-effects distribution were compared: conditional expectation
at $\alpha\widehat\mu_e$ and the mean of conditional expectations over
$\mathcal N(\alpha\widehat\mu_e,\alpha^2\widehat\tau_e^2)$.

The reanalysis retained the original 12-donor calibration, 24-donor pilot,
and 56-donor recipient split. The pilot selected $\alpha$ separately for
each prediction rule from $\{0.5,0.75,1,1.25\}$; final fits used all
36 source donors. Predictions with $\alpha=1$ were also retained. The
hierarchy used its original penalties, and the common-ridge comparison
used the same population penalty of 0.01. All comparisons are post hoc.

Predictive upper-left-count intervals were equal-tail intervals from the
conditional or mixture law. Coverage and interval width were averaged over
informative pairs within each recipient, then equally over recipients.
The fitted source parameters were held fixed. Paired bootstrap intervals for
loss comparisons used 20,000 physical-donor resamples.

Each of 128 seeded replicates contained 12 source donors, 12 recipients, 16 independent binary entity tables per donor, and 128 observations per table. Counts followed the Fisher noncentral-hypergeometric distribution at known population log odds. Source donor log odds either equaled the population value or had independent normal deviations with standard deviation 1.2; recipient log odds equaled the population value without donor deviations. Recipient margins were source-like or shifted toward opposing row and column prevalences, using common random streams across margin conditions.

\subsection{Transfer-risk stress test}

The stress test fixed the source field at its known value and crossed bounded mean-zero recipient drift, transport multipliers $\{0.5,0.75,1,1.25\}$, drift standard deviations $\{0,0.25,0.5,1\}$, and three informative margins. Finite-support summation gave the expected conditional KL risk, numerical quadrature evaluated its curvature-integral form, and $10^6$ seeded conditional draws per drift state gave an independent Monte Carlo estimate.

All \RiskStressEnvelopePass{} conditions satisfied the Fisher-information and finite-support envelopes. Exact KL and its curvature integral differed by at most $\RiskStressIntegralError{}$. Monte Carlo intervals covered the exact risk in \RiskStressCoverage{} conditions, with maximum absolute $z=\RiskStressMaxZ$. At $\alpha=1$, increasing the drift standard deviation from 0 to 1 raised count-level expected log loss from 0 to 2.835 for balanced margins, 1.504 for row-sparse margins, and 1.322 for overlap-shifted margins; each table contained 96 observations.

\section{Secondary multistate methods}

\subsection{Permutation-centered fields}

For linked state vectors $(U_i,V_i)_{i=1}^n$, let $h_\alpha(U,V)$ denote Eq.~\eqref{eq:s-field} applied to the joint count table after adding pseudocount $\alpha$. With $B$ independent permutations of the $V$ labels, the centered field is
\begin{equation}
\widehat\field=h_\alpha(U,V)-B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B h_\alpha(U,V^{(b)}).
\label{eq:s-permutation-field}
\end{equation}
Under the fixed-margin link null, observed and permuted matchings are identically distributed conditional on both label multisets, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:s-permutation-field} has conditional mean zero. If $S^2$ is the coordinatewise sample variance of the $B$ reference fields, $(1+1/B)S^2$ is an unbiased estimator of the variance of an observed-minus-reference field. Multistate analyses use $B=64$, $\alpha=0.5$, and one independent destroyed-link permutation.

For perturbation $g$ and control $0$ under challenge $1$ and vehicle $0$, the factorial field is $(\field_{g1}-\field_{g0})-(\field_{01}-\field_{00})$. Independent arm-permutation variances are added before converting them to clipped relative precision,
\[
w_{gj}=\operatorname{clip}\left(
\frac{1/v_{gj}}{\operatorname{median}_{g,j}(1/v_{gj})},0.05,20
\right).
\]

\subsection{Structured field estimator}

For target-by-coordinate matrix $Y$, positive weight matrix $W$, positive-semidefinite hypergraph Laplacian $L_H$, and nonnegative penalties, the estimator minimizes
\begin{equation}
 F_Y(M)=\frac12\norm{Y-M}_{W}^{2}
 +\lambda_*\norm{M}_*+\lambda_G\operatorname{tr}(M^{\mathsf T}L_HM),
 \label{eq:s-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\norm{A}_W^2=\sum_{g,j}W_{gj}A_{gj}^2$. The normalized hypergraph Laplacian is
\[
L_H=I-D_v^{-1/2}HD_e^{-1}W_eH^{\mathsf T}D_v^{-1/2}.
\]
Isolated vertices receive zero rows and columns and are unaffected by the graph penalty.

Each covered target formed an unweighted hyperedge with its five highest-cosine covered neighbors. The benchmark used frozen scGPT and gene2vec embeddings; label-permuted scGPT supplied the shuffled-graph control, and targets absent from an embedding were isolated. In PerturbSci, Frangieh, and Papalexi rotations, the other two units selected penalties by symmetric cross-prediction mean squared error. The nuclear penalty was a fraction of the largest training-matrix singular value, on grid $\{0,0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3,0.6,1\}$; the graph grid was $\{0,0.05,0.2,1,5,20\}$. Nuclear-only, graph-only, and joint fits used the corresponding grids. The fixed variance-weighted fit used nuclear fraction 0.1 and graph penalty 5; scalar shrinkage used 26 multipliers from 0 to 1.25, and endpoint ridge used $\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},0.1,1,10,100\}$. MultiPerturb, PerturbFate, and Arce used the same five-neighbor graph and fixed penalties.

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness and nonexpansive stability]
If $\min_{g,j}W_{gj}>0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:s-objective} has a unique minimizer. For two input matrices $Y_1,Y_2$ with the same weights, hypergraph, and penalties, their minimizers satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\widehat M_1-\widehat M_2}_W
  \le \norm{Y_1-Y_2}_W.
  \label{eq:nonexpansive}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The weighted quadratic loss is strongly convex and both penalties are convex, so their sum has a unique minimizer. Let $R$ denote the combined penalty and choose $G_k\in\partial R(\widehat M_k)$. From $W\odot(\widehat M_k-Y_k)+G_k=0$, subtracting the two optimality equations and taking the Frobenius inner product with $\widehat M_1-\widehat M_2$ gives, by monotonicity of the convex subdifferential,
\[
\norm{\widehat M_1-\widehat M_2}_W^2
\le \langle W\odot(Y_1-Y_2),\widehat M_1-\widehat M_2\rangle.
\]
Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:nonexpansive}.
\end{proof}

Accelerated proximal gradient descent soft-thresholds singular values in the proximal step and handles the weighted residual and graph quadratic form in the smooth gradient. Iteration stops at a relative Frobenius step below $10^{-8}$; nonconverged estimates are rejected.

\subsection{GSE317605 longitudinal calibration}

GSE317605 profiled longitudinal PBMC RNA and surface proteins during a phase II trial of pembrolizumab plus granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor in advanced biliary cancer \cite{kelley2026}. The split, fixed before assay-value access, assigned seven complete patients to calibration, three complete patients to the pilot, and eight patients to held evaluation. Five held patients had complete visit series and three had partial series. Each calibration visit retained 192 cells selected independently of assay counts and 256 ordered pairs from 16 matched RNA and ADT markers.

For visit $v$ at time $t(v)$ and marker pair $e$, the longitudinal model decomposed the log odds into a time-specific population field $\beta_{t(v),e}$ and visit deviation $u_{ve}$. With $V_t$ denoting visits at time $t$, it minimized
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{long}}={}&\sum_t\frac{1}{|V_t|}\sum_{v\in V_t}
\left\{\sum_e\ell_{ve}(\beta_{te}+u_{ve})
+\frac{\eta}{2}\norm{u_v}_2^2\right\}
+\frac{0.01}{2}\norm{\beta}_F^2\\
&+\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_t\beta_t^{\mathsf T}L_{\mathrm{pair}}\beta_t
+\frac{\tau}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{3}\norm{\beta_{t+1}-\beta_t}_2^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s-longitudinal-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_{ve}$ is the exact fixed-margin negative log likelihood. The marker operator was $L_{\mathrm{pair}}=L_H\otimes I+I\otimes L_H$, with $L_H$ the normalized Laplacian of a fixed marker hypergraph; temporal smoothing followed the path from T01 to T04. The direct comparator fitted one time-specific interaction per pair by ridge-penalized profiled Poisson likelihood, then profiled row and column terms at each target margin. Both methods used identical patient-disjoint folds and transport grids. Loss was averaged across pairs within each visit, then across visits within each patient, with patients weighted equally.

Leave-one-patient-out calibration crossed donor-deviation penalties $\{0.1,1,10\}$, hypergraph and temporal penalties $\{0,0.05,0.2\}$, and transport multipliers $\{0,0.5,0.75,1,1.25\}$. The matched classical comparator crossed ridge penalties $\{0.001,0.01,0.1,1\}$ with the same transport grid. The selected field fit used deviation penalty 0.1, temporal penalty 0.2, hypergraph penalty zero, and transport 0.75. The Poisson fit selected ridge penalty 1 and transport 0.75. Mean deviance was \GSEThreeOneSevenPrimaryLoss{}, compared with \GSEThreeOneSevenPoissonLoss{} for Poisson prediction. The reduction was \GSEThreeOneSevenPoissonReduction{}, with favorable results in \GSEThreeOneSevenPoissonFav{} patients and \GSEThreeOneSevenTimeFav{} time points. Relative to destroyed links, deviance fell by \GSEThreeOneSevenDestroyedReduction{}, with favorable results in \GSEThreeOneSevenDestroyedFav{} patients. Progression required at least 5\% improvement over Poisson and strict improvement over a separately retuned graph-zero fit. The primary and graph-zero fits were identical, so the calibration criterion was not met and the pilot and held assay values were not accessed.

\section{Limitations}

The 81 within-donor pair tables reuse cells and marker vectors, but the composite objective does not model their joint covariance. Held-donor bootstraps condition on the fitted source field, selected configuration, and deterministic cell subsamples. The original within-donor median ranks fixed every Stephenson protein margin at 256/256, including two recipients with almost no counts across the selected protein panel. The source-fixed threshold analysis permits protein-margin variation but does not establish biologically validated positivity thresholds. Both this analysis and the selected-panel exclusion followed outcome access.

Each primary destroyed-link comparison uses one deterministic within-donor permutation; the repeated-permutation sensitivity retained 29 of 32 fits, and three did not meet the convergence criterion. The eight within-cell-type permutations address a different null, preserving broad cell composition. Neither predictive interval family attained nominal 95\% coverage. These intervals treat fitted interaction distributions as known and assume their transportability; they omit source-parameter uncertainty. The simulations use 16 independent entity tables and deliberately strong margin shift. Recipient interactions equal the population parameter in the original four scenarios and vary around it in the extension. Neither simulation defines a multivariate cell-level generative law.

Conditional likelihood removes row and column nuisance parameters but can lose finite-sample information about the odds ratio when one or both margins are random under the sampling design. The lost fraction tends to zero under standard large-sample conditions~\cite{schervish2025}. We did not estimate its magnitude in the sparse marker tables used here.

The original GSE239452 statistic inversion mishandled negative values near the lower feasible endpoint, producing 80 coordinate mismatches among 729 reconstructed tables. The corrected inverse produced none at tolerance $10^{-8}$ and maximum absolute coordinate error below $4.0\times10^{-12}$. It recomputed only statistic-transfer predictions; primary and destroyed-link predictions and held tables were unchanged. Because repair followed outcome access, GSE239452 is a post-access corrected replication, not a second pre-outcome confirmation.

\section{Implementation and reproducibility}
\label{sec:provenance}

The \href{https://github.com/sushaan-k/coupling-fields-benchmark/releases/tag/coupling-fields-v2.0.4-public-benchmark}{v2.0.4 public release} contains the MIT-licensed estimator, analysis protocols, cohort and simulation results, study records, and method grids. Its verification tests reproduce the reported tables and figures from the derived results. Raw matrices remain available from the accessions in Tables~\ref{tab:s-data} and~\ref{tab:s-multistate-data} under their source licenses and are not redistributed.

The reanalysis uses the original 92 donor samples, 512 selected cells per donor, and nine markers per modality. Recovering their selected counts from the public source reproduced all 56 primary held-donor losses to a maximum absolute difference of $5.7\times10^{-15}$. Released aggregate contingency tables reproduce the biological sensitivities without downloading cell-level data. Assay-quality diagnostics and the unchanged original prediction losses reproduce the selected-panel exclusion. The analysis specification and computational provenance distinguish these post hoc analyses from the original held-site protocol.

The recipient-heterogeneity simulation reproduced the original zero-variance losses within $7.0\times10^{-18}$; 40- and 80-node quadrature means agreed within $8.7\times10^{-13}$. Tests check predictive mixtures against independent adaptive integration, degenerate margins, zero variance, convergence failure, and the distribution-to-table regret bound.

The classical random-effects implementation uses \texttt{metafor} 5.0.1's exact conditional model, \texttt{CM.EL}, with no count correction. Tables with singleton support contribute likelihood one and were omitted from the optimizer input to avoid undefined initialization offsets. Fits were compared at two integration tolerances and checked against independent finite-support likelihood calculations. Identical informative donor tables admit an analytic zero-variance solution. Other near-boundary fits used additional optimizers, the one-sided variance score, and a fixed variance profile; this profile is a numerical check, not a global-optimality proof. An initializer retry changed only the preliminary approximate fit. Archived source versions identify retained fits across these numerical repairs. Predictive intervals do not use the ancillary parameter standard errors. For the common-ridge calibration fit, solving the score equations recovered the same strictly convex objective after optimizer nonconvergence, with maximum absolute gradient $9.2\times10^{-11}$; the penalty and selection grid were unchanged.
